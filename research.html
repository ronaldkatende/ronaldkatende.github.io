Research

A common mathematics behind constrained learning.

The programme studies what remains possible when standard assumptions of abundant labels, dense sensors, unlimited computation, or complete physical information are removed.

Pillar 1

Learning under information constraints

Core question: what information must a learner observe before reliable prediction or recovery is possible?

Label complexity · observation models · representation certificates · information geometry · lower bounds.

Pillar 2

Scientific machine learning and inverse problems

Core question: how should physical structure, numerical analysis, and data interact when measurements are sparse or indirect?

Physics-informed learning · PDEs · sparse sensors · residual certification · digital twins.

Pillar 3

Computation, complexity and representation

Core question: which forms of computation require depth, width, sequential work, or genuinely nonlocal information?

Work-span frontiers · circuit lower bounds · nonlocal witnesses · architectural limits.

Pillar 4

Decision-ready AI under resource constraints

Core question: when is a model sufficiently supported to change a real decision rather than merely improve a benchmark?

No-harm selection · evidence efficiency · constrained forecasting · robustness · deployment.

Unifying viewpoint

Across these areas, the recurring object is a constrained information-to-decision pipeline. A system receives an incomplete view of a phenomenon, constructs a representation, performs computation, and returns an estimate, forecast, certificate, or action.

The central task is to separate what is genuinely possible from what only appears possible because an experiment used favourable data, hidden resources, or assumptions that will not survive deployment.

Methods

The programme combines theorem-driven analysis, numerical methods, scientific computing, and carefully scoped empirical work. Computational experiments are used to test specific claims, not to replace mathematical explanation.
