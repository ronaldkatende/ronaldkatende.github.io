Research programme

Stability. Information.
Reliable computation.

A common theme across my work is the gap between what a method can compute and what its available information can justify.

01Scientific machine learning

When can learned numerical methods be trusted?

I study the mathematical properties of learning-based solvers for differential equations, including stability, error bounds, consistency and connections with classical numerical methods.

Recent work examines physics-informed neural networks using operator analysis, and earlier work considers their relationship with finite-element methods and ReLU approximation.

02Inverse problems & sensing

How much can limited observations reveal?

Inverse problems are often defined by what cannot be observed directly. I work on reconstruction from sparse measurements, reduced-order models and numerical conditioning—especially for physical systems where sensing or computation is expensive.

Related directions include condition-aware sensor selection and full-field recovery for industrial digital twins.

03Learning, information & computation

What are the actual limits of learning and computation?

This programme considers memory, representation, measurement and computational work as mathematical resources. One line examines information contraction and stability under nonstandard memory clocks.

Other ongoing investigations concern task-relative numerical linear algebra and the information required to recover or certify a result.

Related paper: information contraction ↗
04Applied mathematical modelling

Can modest models support meaningful decisions?

My applied work uses mathematical and computational modelling to study epidemics, climate resilience, economic transformation, conflict and infrastructure. Across domains, the concern is to keep inference proportionate to the available evidence.

Approach

Clear assumptions. Verifiable claims. Reproducible experiments.

The goal is not simply to obtain a prediction, but to understand the conditions under which it can be trusted.
