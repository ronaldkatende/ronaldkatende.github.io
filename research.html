Research programme

Mathematics for reliable scientific learning

My central question is what information, mathematical structure and computation are enough to support a reliable scientific conclusion—not merely a plausible numerical output.

Research approach

Specify the task. State the assumptions. Prove what is possible. Test what fails. Report limitations.

01 / MATHEMATICAL FOUNDATIONS

Approximation, stability and numerical PDEs

My foundational work is in numerical analysis: how discretization, finite-dimensional approximation, learned representations and solver choices affect the correctness of a computed solution. My PhD investigated the numerical analysis of physics-informed neural networks for partial differential equations.

Published work includes operator-based stability and consistency analysis for PINNs, connections between one-dimensional finite elements and ReLU networks, and error-bounded matrix compression for scientific learning.

02 / INFORMATION & OBSERVATION

What can sparse measurements actually determine?

A prediction may be precise yet unsupported by the measurements available. I study limitations on inference from incomplete observations, the role of task-relevant information, and methods that seek useful reconstructions without demanding full observation of a system.

Current publications address sparse-sensor full-field recovery for industrial digital twins, resource-aware forecasting and information-limited coverage in scientific machine learning.

03 / SCIENTIFIC DECISIONS

When does a model justify action?

Useful scientific computation often serves a particular decision: whether a model can be trusted, which measurement to collect, when to issue a warning, or whether a numerical approximation is sufficient. I am interested in mathematical and computational criteria for making these choices under resource constraints.

This includes prospective evaluation, uncertainty-aware prediction and the distinction between a demonstrated numerical result and a validated operational decision. Applications in infrastructure, health, climate and development provide motivating settings, not automatic evidence of field effectiveness.

Research integrity

The standard I aim for

Theory. Assumptions, identifiability, error and stability bounds stated precisely, with counterexamples where needed.

Computation. Transparent numerical experiments, competitive baselines, ablations and reproducibility checks.

Evidence. Clear separation between simulation, retrospective tests, and prospective or field validation.
