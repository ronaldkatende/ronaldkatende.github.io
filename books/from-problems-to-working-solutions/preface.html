Before we begin

A working solution is often modest. A better medicine ordering rule, a dependable timetable, a repaired measurement process, or an honest calculation that rules out a tempting investment may matter more than a complicated algorithm. But modest does not mean careless. Every recommendation still needs assumptions that can be examined, calculations that can be repeated, and a clear account of whom the decision affects.

The book begins with the problem, not the tool. It moves from observing a service to modelling it, judging the evidence, comparing actions, and finally testing whether the chosen intervention makes a difference. The settings come largely from places where money, reliable power, laboratory access or specialised computing are limited. The reasoning applies anywhere resources and evidence matter.

The book distinguishes a theorem proved from assumptions, an illustrative calculation, an analysis of observed data, and a field-tested intervention. They are not interchangeable. Readers should carry that distinction through every chapter, even when a figure looks convincing.

The Python programs are learning companions rather than black-box applications. They can run on ordinary computers. The online chapters may be read without running any code, and the source archive contains the scripts and downloadable notebooks for students and instructors.
